Tier 1 — Quick Look

One brain got trained, and choosing which save to keep was the whole game

This project has exactly one trained brain, so there is no field of rival AIs to race against each other. What there is — and it is more useful — is a clear record of how that brain developed, and of the decision that nearly went the wrong way: two candidate saves from the same run, one that scored better and one that actually fought better.

The headline for a producer: the fighters are cheap. Ten of them, each running a neural network ten times a second, leave the GPU essentially idle and the CPU frame under half of a 60 fps budget. Performance is not what limits this game.

Tier 2 — Success vs Time

How quickly each fighter learned its job

The choice that mattered

Same training run, two saved checkpoints. In a ten-way free-for-all every fighter's win rate is 10% by construction, so the useful measure is whether a match reaches a real finish or just runs out of clock.

Picked on finishes — shipped as ffa_v5 76%
Picked on score — rejected 21%

Finishing a match early truncates the episode, capping how much damage-reward can accumulate — so the reward function mildly punishes winning quickly. Score and the objective point in opposite directions here.

Learning curve — the ten-way stage, run ffa_v3

Two charts, not one with two scales. They cover the same window of the same run, and the point is how differently they move.

This pair is the single most important thing on the page. A fighter that went from never closing out a match to closing out one in three moved its score by 8%. Anyone watching the scoreboard would have concluded the run had flatlined and killed it.

Tier 2 — Energy & Smoothness

Which fighters move naturally, and which burn themselves out

Stamina — measured

A fighter spends breath on punching and moving and gets it back over time. Under constant punching the system settles at an equilibrium, and that number is the honest measure of how hard the fighters are allowed to work.

Equilibrium under constant punching 0.20
Equilibrium before the alternating-arm rule ~0.24
Punch throughput, measured 2.2 / s

A rule change quietly broke this once. Restricting fighters to one fist out at a time halved punch throughput to about 2.2/sec — which meant that at the old stamina cost of 0.035, the drain (0.077/s) fell below the recovery (0.09/s). Spamming punches literally gained you breath. The cost was doubled to 0.07 to put the pressure back.

Smoothness — what actually caused jitter here

There is Not measured no jitter metric in this project — nobody has logged joint velocity or torque variance. What exists instead is a record of the two things that made the arms visibly shake, both now designed out:

CauseWhat it looked likeResolution
Colliders on the arm segments Limbs came off and scattered across the ring. The dynamic arm chain hangs off a kinematic torso, which is infinitely massive to the solver — an arm pinched between two closing bodies has nowhere to go Upper arms and forearms carry bodies but no colliders, permanently. Only the gloves have presence
Capsule colliders, second attempt Arms visibly oscillated. Every cross-fighter touch pushed a limb while the servo drove back against it, so the drawn pose became a product of contact forces rather than of the model Reverted. Blocking needs no physical presence — it is judged against the line from shoulder to glove
Servo gain too low for the folded guard Fists visibly fell short — measured 1.53 against a 1.60 reach Folding the guard made the arm slew 117° instead of 37° in the same window, so servo gain went from 25 to 60
Nested arm rigidbodies Jointed limbs drift — a nested body is moved twice, once by physics and once by the hierarchy Arm segments are siblings of the torso, never children

On realism: joint limits are anatomical — the elbow cannot hyperextend, the wrist is held to ±25°, the shoulder to a human range. Gravity scale is 0 because this is a top-down view and Earth gravity points into the screen; there is no in-plane fall to simulate, and no floaty scaling is being hidden by it.

StepWhy
Start TensorBoard before the trainer, and verify the port answersProject rule. Start-Process reports success even when the process dies a second later, so its exit status proves nothing

Preserve checkpoints across the whole runThe good save is only identifiable at the end, and this project has already lost one to rotation
Close the Editor for runs over 30 minutesGive the run the machine
