PoRumble — Level & Arena Setup

Where everything sits in the ring, in real world units, read from the scene files on 2026-09-12.

Tier 1 — Quick Look

A square ring, ten chairs around the edge, four walls and no obstacles

The playfield is one square room. Ten fighters start evenly spaced around a circle, each roughly facing the middle, and walk inward until somebody is in reach. There is nothing in the middle — no cover, no hazards, no pickups. Everything you can see other than the fighters and the four walls is decoration: posts, stools, the ring logo, the crowd.

The ring is sized against a real one. A professional ring is 20 feet inside the ropes, and this one is 19.9 feet. It was nearly 47 feet across for a long time — most of a tennis court — which is why ten fighters used to read as specks who spent the first half of every match walking toward each other.

Ring interior 17 × 17 units = 6.07 × 6.07 m 10 spawn points on a circle of radius 6.375 Decoration, no collision
Tier 2 — Core Mechanics

SampleScene — the game arena, seen from above

The walls do not contain anybody. Fighter positions are written from the model every tick, so a wall collider never gets a chance to stop one — BoxerSystem.ClampToArena is what actually holds them in, using _arenaHalfExtent (8.5). If the drawn ring and that number ever disagree, fighters stop short of the ropes or walk straight through them, and nothing is logged either way.

How the ten chairs are filled

The spawn transforms above are the slots, not the final positions. Every episode the whole ring is rotated by a random amount and each fighter is jittered off its slot, so no two matches open identically — replaying one fixed opening for millions of steps teaches a policy that opening rather than the game.

RandomisationRangeWhy
Whole-ring rotation0–360°Applied once per episode to every slot at once
Slot jitter±12°Slots are evenly spaced before jitter, which is what guarantees the separation
Radius jitter±18%In or out of the spawn circle
Facing jitter±35°Without it the opponent is always dead ahead on step one and the policy never learns to find one

The generator is a seeded xorshift owned by the spawn system, not UnityEngine.Random — a global generator would make every training episode depend on whatever else happened to draw a number that frame.

Trigger zones and hit volumes — around a single fighter

FaceProbe must stay a trigger. It is much larger than the drawn head — 0.80 against 0.30 — because it is the perception volume as well as the hit volume. Make it solid and it stops being a sensor and starts shoving fighters around at nearly a metre of reach.

The three arenas, at one scale

Flagged 2026-09-12. The game ring was rescaled from 40×40 to 17×17 and Training10Way was not rescaled with it. The shipped brain learned its distances — when to close, when to hold range, how long a wall is — on a field with more than four times the area. This is the most likely source of odd spacing on screen, and rescaling means moving the drawn ring and BoxerSpawnPoints._arenaHalfExtent together.

Flagged 2026-09-12 — the walls are untagged in two of the three scenes. SampleScene and Training10Way leave all four walls Untagged, while the ray sensor lists Wall as its third detectable tag. The rays still stop on a wall, so a fighter senses an obstruction — it just cannot tell that the obstruction is a rope rather than an unknown. What partly covers for this is the position-in-ring observation, which was added precisely because rays cannot say "you are in a corner". Tagging the walls in those two scenes is a one-field fix; note that it changes what the sensor reports, so the brain should be re-checked against it.

Layers and the collision matrix

Ten layers, BoxerBody0 through BoxerBody9, one per roster slot. At spawn, BoxerSpawnPoints.IsolatePerception moves every collider belonging to fighter n onto BoxerBody{n} and subtracts that layer from that fighter's own ray mask.

PairingCollides?Why
BoxerBody{n} ↔ itselfNoA fighter's own parts never collide with each other. A glove tucked to the face sits inside the torso collider, and the servo would fight a contact it can never win
BoxerBody{n} ↔ BoxerBody{m}Yes — all 45 pairsFighters collide with each other normally
Any boxer layer ↔ DefaultYesThe walls live on Default
Ray mask, fighter nEverything except BoxerBody{n}So a fighter can never perceive itself

The single nastiest failure in the project. A 2D ray cannot skip the collider it started inside. Two things keep the sensor honest and both are load-bearing: project-wide Physics2D > Queries Start In Colliders must be off, and IsolatePerception must run. Turn off either and every forward ray — the ones pointing exactly where the fighter is about to punch — reports that fighter's own face at half a metre, permanently, with no error anywhere. PerceptionSettingsTests pins the first half.

Build scene objects through the editor, not from code. Anything static — positions, props, rig objects — should exist in the scene as an asset that can be dragged. Code that spawns static scenery at runtime takes away the ability to adjust it. Everything in the tables above is authored in the scene.
