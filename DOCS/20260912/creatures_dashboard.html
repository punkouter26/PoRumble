Tier 1 — Quick Look

One brain, nine bodies, ten decisions a second

Every AI boxer in the ring runs the same 475 KB trained brain. What makes BIGGIE fight differently from EPSTEIN is a thin style layer that bends that shared answer on its way to the body, plus four attributes — power, chin, speed, recovery. One seat is a hand-written opponent that is not AI at all, and it is always red.

The brain is finished and shipping. It sees with 17 feelers and 15 facts about itself, decides ten times a second, and is paid for landing punches. It was chosen on how often its matches actually finish — not on its score, which in this project points the wrong way.

Tier 2 — Executive scorecard

How good is the fighter, really?

The number that decided which brain shipped

In a ten-way free-for-all every fighter's win rate is 10% by construction, so win rate says nothing. The useful question is whether a match reaches a real finish or just runs out of clock. Two candidate brains, same run:

Chosen on finishes — shipped 76%
Chosen on score — rejected 21%

Score and the goal pull in opposite directions here. Finishing a match early cuts the episode short, which caps how much damage-reward can pile up — so the reward function mildly punishes winning quickly. Picking the best-scoring save would have shipped the 21% fighter.

The two caveats, in one line each

Ring size: the brain trained in a 40×40 ring and now fights in a 17×17 one — the game ring was rescaled and the ten-way training scene was not. Distances it learned do not mean the same thing on screen.
Arm blocking: after training, blocking changed from "did the fist meet a glove" to "did the fist cross the whole arm". The shipped brain still throws punches that used to land and now get blocked. Accepted as retraining debt; the controls were untouched, so the file still loads and still plays.

The four scripted difficulty tiers

The red opponent is really four. Reaction delay is the honest difficulty dial — it is how long the bot is not allowed to respond to something it can already see.

Only one of these four is currently in play. SampleScene has the full card of eight contestants assigned, and a card replaces the difficulty-tier path outright — so the scene deals 2 scripted seats and 8 policy seats, and the scripted ones are HEURISTIC, which points at Journeyman. Tier 3 — Rig

Joint limits and degrees of freedom

Every contestant — human, scripted and trained — wears the same body: 7 rigidbodies, 6 hinge joints, one degree of freedom each. Scale is fixed by the drawn fighter being 1.40 units across the shoulders against a real boxer's 0.50 m, so 1 world unit = 0.357 m.

Do not give the upper arms or forearms colliders. Tried twice. The arm chain is dynamic and hangs off a kinematic torso, which is infinitely massive to the solver — an arm caught between two closing bodies has nowhere to go and is ejected across the ring, hinge chain and all. Blocking does not need them: it is judged against the line from shoulder to glove.

Gravity scale is 0 on every body. This is a top-down view, so Earth gravity points into the screen — there is no in-plane fall to simulate. Masses are ratios tuned so the hinge servos settle rather than oscillate, not kilograms.

Dodge duration must stay above arm extend duration. A fighter cannot slip earlier than the moment it sees an arm start to travel, so a window shorter than the punch's flight time closes before the punch arrives and the mechanic does nothing at all.
