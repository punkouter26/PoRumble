PoRumble — a second reading

What the fighter wants

The same 119,313 numbers that play the game learned it by wanting things. This is the shape of that wanting.

Tier 1 — thirty seconds

It wants to be the last one standing

Everything else is instrumental. Finding someone, closing to arm's length, hitting a face rather than a guard, not being hit back — none of these are goals. They are what winning decomposes into, and the agent is paid for them only until it stops needing the help.

Tier 2 — the two clocks

Wanting that expires, and wanting that does not

Three of the rewards are scaffolding. They pay per step for facing the nearest opponent, for walking toward them, and for standing at punching range — exactly the right things to reward when a fighter cannot yet find anybody, and exactly the wrong things once it can. Hovering at reach without throwing scores almost as well as fighting.

So the scaffolding is switched off on a schedule. shaping_scale steps 1.0 → 0.5 → 0.0 across the first 30% of a run, and what is left is the fight.
the bell
500 decisions. An episode is 2,500 physics steps at a decision every 5. Standing still costs −1.0 spread across all of them, so idling is never free.
the horizon
γ = 0.997. The win is still worth 0.22 at the opening bell. At 0.995 it was worth 0.08 — too faint to shape the early game, which is why the policy optimised the dense terms instead.
memory
256 steps. Below the discount horizon the value estimate bootstraps before the terminal reward can reach it. It was 128.

Tier 2 — two regimes

Training is not playing

Training

  • Eight rings at once, 80 units apart — closer and a fighter sees the fight next door
  • Arms posed from the model, solver off: 60 bodies and 60 joints per ring that decide nothing
  • No camera, no HUD, no audio — every presentation component is optional and absent
  • Episode ends and re-racks roughly every 50 seconds of simulated time
  • It is allowed to be wrong. Being wrong is the data

Playing

  • One ring, ten seats, a fight card that can be re-dealt between matches
  • Arms on real hinge joints, servoed every physics step
  • A StyleModulator bends its choices into a personality
  • An Elo table remembers across sessions
  • It gets one go

Tier 3 — the loop underneath

How wanting becomes weights

The agent acts, remembers a window of what happened, compares it against what it expected, and adjusts toward whatever beat the expectation. PPO's clip keeps each adjustment small enough that one lucky round cannot rewrite the fighter.

observe
17 rays across a 180° forward arc plus 20 self scalars, stacked twice. It is blind behind itself, deliberately — so is a boxer.
decide
Two dense layers of 256. Four continuous outputs and two binary ones. No memory, no recurrence — memory_size is 0.
act
Move, aim, and one switch per fist. The haymaker and the slip are not actions — they ride side channels, because growing the action vector stops any compiled policy loading.
consequence
Resolved in pure maths, never a physics query, so the same inputs always give the same fight. Reinforcement learning depends on that.
credit
Damage, blocks, slips and clashes land immediately; eliminations and the win land at the end. 78% of a clean sweep's return is the outcome, not the damage.
One thing it must learn that nothing tells it: both fists travel to the same centreline, so throwing them together makes them collide. The punch is lost and both arms are knocked into recovery. Nothing forbids it — it simply costs.
